About Ethereum Town

A miniature town where Ethereum's activity is the traffic. When the live feed is available, every train is a real block that was just produced on mainnet, about half a minute behind the chain, with its real transactions, blobs and burned ETH. Without it, or when you fast-forward, the trains replay three hours of real blocks from the morning of Sep 28, 2026. Click any building to see what it is, its latest numbers and links to the sources behind them.

How to read it

Block trainOne real block every 12 seconds. Coach windows light up with the block's transaction count. Each container on the flatcars is one blob, colored by the L2 that posted it.
L2 shopsThe 14 L2s with the best average of their activity rank and value-secured rank on L2BEAT (7-day averages); the best gets the lot closest to the station. The rosette on each roof is L2BEAT's five-part risk summary: green is good, amber a caution, red a risk. Building height grows with the square root of value secured (not counting the chain's own token, such as ARB or OP), and the crowd at the door with the square root of activity. Colors follow each network's brand; there are no logos.
Builders' RowAcross the tracks, the block builders, sized by their share of recent blocks as tagged in each block. The workshop that built the arriving block lights up and sends it over. Self-built blocks come from the proposing validator's own node.
Blob trucksOne truck for each L2 that posted blobs in that block, leaving from its shop. Batches from L2s outside the top 14, or from unlabeled posters, arrive from out of town.
CouriersL2s that publish their data somewhere other than Ethereum (EigenDA, or their own network) send small couriers instead of trucks. Their timing is illustrative.
Beacon VaultStaking. The people lined up outside are ETH waiting to be staked, about 20,000 ETH per figure; the line's length is the real entry queue, and the front walks in through the IN door at a sped-up pace. The short row by the OUT door is the exit queue. Gold sparks are real reward payouts: each block carries up to 16 withdrawals, and the sparks fly to the homes on the hill, which stand in for everyone who stakes. A figure leaving through the OUT door marks a withdrawal of 1 ETH or more, usually stake leaving, and the vault glows when an epoch's attestation rewards are credited.
The BurnFlares with the base fee burned by each block. The supply counter adds new issuance every slot and subtracts each block's burn.
The MintStablecoins on Ethereum mainnet. The coin stacks are the five largest dollar stablecoins plus everything else, sized by supply.

Where the numbers come from

  • Blocks, gas, base fee and blob counts: a public Ethereum node (JSON-RPC). The replay uses blocks 26,074,816 to 26,075,715, and the station card links each block to Etherscan so you can check it.
  • L2 activity (user operations per second), value secured and stages: L2BEAT, 7-day averages. Value secured leaves out each chain's own token. Projects L2BEAT lists under “Others” are shown as Not rated.
  • Blob attribution by L2: Blobscan, matched to L2BEAT batch-poster addresses where unlabeled.
  • Block builders and validator payouts: each block's extra-data tag and withdrawals, read from the same public node. Tags are self-reported.
  • Staking and queues: validatorqueue.com (beaconcha.in data).
  • Issuance, burn and supply: ultrasound.money. Stablecoins and their issuers: DefiLlama.
  • The figures and explainers were checked against these sources on Sep 28, 2026.

What's illustrative

People, cars, courier timing and the exact truck routes are decoration scaled from the data. Numbers in the cards are real and dated, and estimates are labeled as estimates. Nothing here is investment advice.
